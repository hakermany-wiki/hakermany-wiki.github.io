Haven of Thought is a thorough compendium of security related knowledge, with following structure:

![Wiki categories, sections, topics and maintainers](/images/knowledge-structure.png)

- **Category** - Highest level of structure, defines whole areas (e.g. Offensive Security)
- **Section** - Subcategory, breaks down each *Category* into various approaches to the subject (e.g. Domains),
- **Topic** - Topics are separate chapters for each *Section*, they may introduce their own structure if described concept is complex but can be also simple one-pagers,
- **Caretakers** - Core maintainers of *Categories* and *Sections*, they not only share their knowledge but also manage whole structure to ensure Wiki is transparent and logical,
- **Authors** - Essential members, who may contribute to the Wiki by creating new topics and sharing their expertise.

## Offensive Security (Red Team)
This category covers offensive operations: penetration testing, adversary emulation, red teaming, and all tradecraft for breaching security controls, staying undetected, and maintaining target control.

Following sections have been identified for **[Offensive Security Overview](/offensive-security/overview/)**:
- [**Tactics** *Inspired by MITRE ATT&CK, organized by attack stages and objectives (tactics): reconnaissance, initial access, stealthy exploitation, lateral movement, persistence, and beyond.*](/offensive-security/tactics/)
- [**Domains** *Domain-centric approach for each platform (e.g.OS, mobile, web, cloud) including quirks, mechanisms, and unique TTPs that are most efficient for particular environment.*](/offensive-security/domains/)
- [**Resources** *Practical practical toolkit: wikis, payloads, wordlists, docs, repositories and training platforms.*](/offensive-security/resources/)

## Defensive Operations (Blue Team)
This category covers defensive operations: threat detection, incident response, vulnerability management, and hardening to protect systems, networks, and data from attacks.

Following sections have been identified for **[Defensive Operations](/defensive-operations/overview/)**:

- [**Methods**  *Inspired by MITRE D3FEND and organized by response stages: detection, analysis, containment, eradication, recovery, and hardening.*](/defensive-operations/methods/)
- [**Domains** *Domain-centric approach for each platform (e.g. OS, mobile, web, cloud) including monitoring setups, hardening configs and detection rules tailored to environment-specific threats.*](/defensive-operations/domains/)
- [**Resources** *Practical defensive toolkit: SIEM/EDR docs, threat intel feeds, vuln scanners, IR playbooks, repos and training platforms for monitoring, hunting, and response.*](/defensive-operations/resources/)

This category covers the organizational and regulatory side of cybersecurity: asset and risk management, process governanc and compliance obligations that keep things structured and audit-ready.

Following sections have been identified for **[Governance, Risk and Compliance](/grc/overview/)**:

- [**Concepts** *Core standards and practices for asset classification, process governance and control frameworks, including data protection, incident response, risk management and compliance fundamentals.*](/grc/concepts/)
- [**Sectors** *Sector-centric approach for each regulated industry (e.g. FinTech, Healthcare, TelCom/ICT) this section breaks down the specific assets, requirements and know-how that apply to particular organization.*](/grc/sectors/)
- [**Resources** *Practical regulatory toolkit: key regulations, standards, control frameworks and tools used to assess, track, and maintain compliance.*](/grc/resources/)

## Automation
This category covers getting things up and running fast: automation, scripting, ready-to-go tooling, and integrations (plus AI helpers) so you can launch an environment in minutes.

Following sections have been identified for **[Automation](/automation/overview/)**:

- [**Quick Deploy** *Spin things up fast: serverless functions, managed services, containers and quick-spin services, deployment options where the infra overhead is either minimal or abstracted away entirely.*](/automation/quick-deploy/)
- [**DevTools** *The dev toolkit: programming environments, environment setup & redistribution, day-to-day utilities and tooling that keep the workflow efficient.*](/automation/devtools/)
- [**AI** *AI for automation: core concepts, hosted vs managed LLM setups, agents, prompt engineering, and MCP (Model Context Protocol) for integrating LLM tools into external systems and workflows.*](/automation/ai/)
- [**Workflow & Integrations** *System glue: orchestration patterns, APIs, and webhooks for building reliable, event-driven pipelines and service-to-service integrations.*](/automation/workflow-orchestration/)
